# Engine sound credits

The `*-es-*` loops are our own renders, made with engine-sim (MIT) from its
bundled engine scripts; engine-sim is credited below all the same. They are
rendered with `scripts/engine-sim/`, then cut, made to loop by crossfading
each end into its start, equalised and given a low-passed dry sub layer where
`static/rev-samples.js` says so, filtered below 25 Hz, levelled and encoded as
Ogg Opus by `scripts/rev-samples.mjs`. The `*-start` and `*-stop` clips are
renders of the engine starting and stopping (`render-oneshots.sh`); the
start's cranking is mixed with a starter motor we synthesise
(`--oneshots`). The optional extras (`*-long`, `*-part-*`, `*-limiter*`,
`*-shift-*` and the rev points between the others) are renders of the
same engines held longer, at light throttle, on the rev limiter and
through an upshift (`render-extras.sh`), cut and levelled the same way.

## engine-sim by AngeTheGreat

- What: Rendered offline on our own machine with engine-sim, a physically modelled engine simulator, from engine scripts: the engine held on its dynamometer at each rpm at full throttle (on-load) and closed throttle (overrun), plus a free-running idle; heard through engine-sim's own "real engine" impulse response (the engine group) and its "minimal muffling" one (the exhaust group), with a dry render of the same runs as a low sub layer; and each engine started from rest and stopped from idle, its cranking mixed with a starter motor we synthesise. For the optional extras: more rev points, held at light throttle too, and held longer for 4 s loops; the engine free-revving against engine-sim's own rev limiter; and upshifts under load, the ignition cut on the dyno while the revs fall to the next gear. scripts/engine-sim/ rebuilds them.
